# SEO Content Research and Publication Plan — 3 August 2026

## Decision

Publish one tightly connected, research-backed owner-education cluster instead of producing a large batch of unrelated keyword articles. Four new guides expand the existing `SEO` category from one article into a substantive five-article hub. Each guide answers a different task, cites current first-party Google documentation, links to the directory product and avoids ranking guarantees.

This content is supporting work. It does not remove the directory's larger need for reviewed, current business inventory and independent authority signals.

## Content standard applied

Every new guide has:

- one distinct user task and no duplicate search intent;
- at least eight substantive sections and four FAQs;
- a descriptive title, excerpt and meta description written for clicks without superlatives;
- four contextual internal links, including an owner action or related guide;
- at least three current sources, prioritizing official Google documentation for Google product claims;
- a visible disclaimer that setup, citations, content and Search Console do not guarantee rankings;
- an identified editorial desk, publication date and review date;
- no invented prices, ratings, review counts, verification methods or performance claims.

## Publication and measurement plan

1. Deploy the technical SEO and content branch.
2. Confirm `/blog/category/seo` is indexable and included in the blog sitemap.
3. Inspect the category and four guide URLs in Search Console after deployment.
4. Submit the existing sitemap index once; do not repeatedly resubmit it as a ranking tactic.
5. Record a 28-day pre-publication baseline for branded terms, generic directory terms and each new guide's query group.
6. Compare equivalent 28-day and 90-day periods using clicks, impressions, CTR and page/query pairs. Treat average position as an aggregate, not a fixed manual rank.
7. Keep an article only when it serves its intended audience. Improve it from first-party query data and owner questions; do not manufacture freshness by changing dates.

## Work still required outside content

The highest-priority non-content program is to acquire, review and maintain genuine business profiles. Focus first on a limited set of city/category combinations where at least ten qualified profiles can support a useful landing page. Each accepted profile needs a real identity, category, location, contact route, provenance and review date under the central publication policy.

After the product has useful inventory, earn independent mentions through Nepal business associations, chambers, municipal or industry resources, partners, local media and the businesses listed. Do not buy link packages or exchange sitewide keyword links. Authority must reflect a product people actually use and cite.
